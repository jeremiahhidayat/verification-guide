# Chapter 10: Interview Preparation

Verification interviews test three things: whether you understand the *semantics* (not just the
syntax) of SystemVerilog and UVM, whether you can *design* a verification approach for a block on a
whiteboard, and whether you have *judgment* from experience (debug stories, tradeoffs, what you
would do differently). This chapter gives you a question bank with model answers, coding exercises
with solutions, and design challenges with the structure of a strong answer.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| [10.1](01-question-bank.md) | ~120 questions across SV, SVA, coverage, CRV, OOP, UVM, formal, methodology, with concise model answers and the "what they are really asking" note |
| [10.2](02-coding-exercises.md) | Whiteboard coding: assertions, constraints, testbench snippets, UVM components, with solutions and common mistakes |
| [10.3](03-design-challenges.md) | "How would you verify X?" for a FIFO, an arbiter, an APB register block, an AXI-Stream converter, a cache, a DMA, an interrupt controller: structured answers |
| [10.4](04-behavioral-and-stories.md) | Behavioral questions and how to prepare bug stories, disagreement stories, and "what would you change" answers |

How to use: answer each question out loud *before* reading the model answer. Interviewers grade
explanation quality; recitation is obvious and unimpressive.
